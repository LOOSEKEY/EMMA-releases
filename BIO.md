# THE WORKS
### The world, the work, and the working theory behind what I'm building

---

I'm **Greg** — a solo, self-funded software developer from **Rugby, in the United
Kingdom**. On my own, I'm building something most of the industry quietly gave up
on: a complete ecosystem of applications that run entirely on the user's own
machine. No monthly fees. No cloud account. No company standing between a person
and their own data.

Across a growing family of products — a personal AI companion, a professional
collaboration platform, a sovereign field-AI appliance, and a dozen more in build
— one principle holds throughout: **you own it outright, and it works with the
internet unplugged.** I call the whole of it **The Works** — a works is a place
where things get built, and *the works* is all of it. It was the *Greg-Verse* for
a while; that was a joke, and it was starting to look like it wasn't.

---

## The idea

Most modern software is *rented*. You talk to something in a browser, it forgets
you the moment you close the tab, your data lives on somebody else's computer, and
the meter never stops running. Every feature wants a subscription; every serious
capability, we're told, has to live in the cloud.

My position is simple, and I'll state it plainly: **that's a business model, not a
technical necessity.** Local applications are every bit as capable as their cloud
counterparts — and when an app runs locally, the user holds their own data,
always. Not "we take your privacy seriously." The data never leaves the machine
because there is nowhere else for it to go.

From that one conviction comes an unusually ambitious plan: build in *every*
direction, across *every* sector — companionship, professional work, security,
media, creativity, finance, family, even games — as a single **in-house
ecosystem**, and host all of it **from home**. Not one app scrapping for a niche,
but a whole self-owned software world, with no landlord anywhere in it.

I'm also betting on a turn that has already begun. As people grow tired of renting
their own tools and handing over their own data, they're starting to come **full
circle — back to software they own and control.** When they arrive, I intend to be
ready and waiting, with the ecosystem already built.

## The public products

### ◆ EMMA — *ownemma.com*
*A friend who lives on your computer, remembers your life, and never tells anyone.*

EMMA is the heart of The Works: a genuinely personal AI that lives on your
own machine. She remembers you — properly, for years, across whatever model she
happens to be thinking with — and she can actually *do* things on your computer
when you ask. She reads your day out to you in the morning in her own voice, tells
serial bedtime stories she remembers chapter to chapter, reads your local calendar
and email without a single byte leaving the house, and overnight she *dreams* —
joining up things you've said months apart and waking with something to tell you.

Her design carries the idea the whole ecosystem hangs on: **the model is
disposable, EMMA is not.** Her memory, her identity and her judgement live in a
layer *above* whatever language model she runs on, so you can swap the model for
something smarter next year and she's the same EMMA — just sharper. Everything is
encrypted where it sits, with a key only you hold, and every action she takes is
written to a log you can read yourself.

She runs on your own hardware, free for 60 days with no account and no card, and
**£149 once** if you keep her — yours forever, never a subscription. Available for
Windows, macOS and Linux.

### ⚓ Harbour AI — *harbour-ai.co.uk*
My professional-grade counterpart to EMMA: a local-first AI platform built for
real work, with the same uncompromising stance — no cloud dependency, no
telemetry, and your data never leaving your control. Where EMMA is the companion
at the kitchen table, Harbour AI is the one built for the desk, the team, and the
kind of work that can't be handed to someone else's servers. It's live and in
active use.

### 🛰️ CLANK
*A sovereign, offline-first AI for remote and extreme environments.*

CLANK is a finished, installable appliance: a council of specialist AI agents that
debate a problem and reason together over a licensed, dated field library —
**running entirely on your own hardware, and carrying on thinking with the network
completely unplugged.** I built it for exactly the places the cloud can't reach.
Released and downloadable.

## A look at the wider ecosystem

The public products are the visible edge of something much larger. Behind them,
I'm building — in-house, solo — a set of applications that reach into sector after
sector, each one standalone, each one local-first, and many of them quietly
powered by the same EMMA core dropped in like a brick:

- **Build Emma** — a private, offline coding platform where a person or a whole
  team builds real projects, with EMMA resident in the editor.
- **CODE-STORE** — self-hosted code storage: everything a cloud code host gives
  you, kept entirely on your own machine.
- **CORA** — a local-first video editor that can take raw footage and produce the
  finished edit for you.
- **ECHO** — a local-first trading platform, orchestrated by EMMA and a team of
  market agents.
- **EVERGREEN** — an encrypted family memory vault, and a letterbox that works
  across time — everything a family makes, kept in one place they own.
- **NORTHLIGHT** — an open-world survival game, exploring what local AI can do for
  characters that actually remember you.
- **THE BASEMENT** — a creator-first live-streaming platform, built the way it
  should have been.

The through-line is deliberate. **EMMA is the connective tissue** — one being,
reused across many standalone apps, each staying whole on its own, each keeping
its own data isolated so nothing bleeds from one into another. It's less like a
product line and more like a single, self-owned digital world growing outward in
every direction.

## Where it's going

The next chapter is consolidation on my own terms: **pulling the whole ecosystem
in-house and hosting the website from home** — the storefront, the downloads, the
lot — so that sovereignty runs end to end, from my own wall socket to the user's
own machine. No cloud provider, no platform tax, no landlord, at any point in the
chain.

It's a wildly independent way to build software, and a deliberate one. I'm not
trying to beat the big cloud companies at their own game. I'm building a complete
alternative *outside* their walls — and proving that a single developer, with a
clear philosophy and enough discipline, can build an entire self-sustaining
software world from a single desk.

Developers don't build full local ecosystems anymore — everything wants money
every month, or it has to live in the cloud. I know that isn't true. Local apps
are just as good, and this way the user always holds their own data. My plan is
simple: build in every direction, pull it all in-house, host it from home, and
take the local ecosystem to the moon and beyond. People are starting to come full
circle, back to software they own. I'm going to be there waiting, ready.
