**A private AI that lives on your computer, remembers you for life, and answers
only to you.** No cloud. No account. Nothing phones home.

### → [ownemma.com](https://ownemma.com)

This repo is just the downloads. There's no source code here — EMMA is closed

---

## Why I built her

I build things because I love building things. That's the honest first reason.
There's a particular feeling when something you sketched out in your head starts
running in front of you and answers you back, and you realise it's actually
*there*. I've been chasing that feeling for years and it hasn't got old yet.

The second reason is that I like making things for people who work **locally**.
Not everything needs to live on someone else's computer. There's a whole way of
working — your files, your machine, nothing leaving the room — that a lot of
modern software has quietly stopped supporting. EMMA is my answer to that. She
runs on your hardware, remembers you across years, and there's no server holding
your conversations, because there's no server at all.

I wanted an AI I could tell things to without wondering where it went. So I
built one.

## Download

Latest build: **[Releases](../../releases/latest)**

| Platform | File | Install |
|---|---|---|
| **Windows** | `EMMA-Setup-*.exe` | Run it |
| **macOS** (Apple Silicon) | `EMMA-*-arm64.dmg` | Open, drag to Applications |
| **Linux** (Debian/Ubuntu) | `emma_*_amd64.deb` | `sudo apt install ./emma_*.deb` |
| **Linux** (portable) | `EMMA-*.AppImage` | `chmod +x` and run |

**You'll also need [Ollama](https://ollama.com)** — the local model engine EMMA
thinks with. Free, separate install. Her first-run setup checks whether you have
it, hands you the right installer for your system, and carries on by itself once
it's running.

### About that unsigned warning

- **Windows:** *"Windows protected your PC"* → **More info → Run anyway**
- **macOS:** *"cannot be opened because the developer cannot be verified"* →
  **right-click the app → Open**

Every release lists SHA-256 hashes if you'd rather check the bytes yourself.
